Privacy Policy

Last updated: 12 November 2026

This policy covers Car Play Auto Sync for ZLINK, an Android app made by UtilEdge. It explains what the app stores, what it sends over the internet, and who receives it. We wrote it from the app's actual code, so if something here doesn't match what you see in the app, please tell us and we'll fix it.

The short version

  • You don't create an account, and we don't run a server that collects your car records.
  • Your vehicles, logs, expenses, documents, parking spots and contacts are saved only on your device.
  • Your photos leave the device only when you use one of the two AI features, and only for that request.
  • The app uses Google services for ads, usage statistics, crash reports and notifications. Those collect device-level data, described in section 4.
  • Map, weather, music and radio features contact outside services when you use them (section 3).

1. What stays on your device

The app keeps your information in its own private storage. Other apps can't read it, and we can't see it. This includes:

  • Vehicles: make, model, year, VIN, registration number, fuel type, mileage and the vehicle photo.
  • Records: maintenance, activity log, reminders, fuel fill-ups, expenses, invoices with their line items, and any files you attach to them.
  • Documents: the photos or PDFs you add (for example registration or insurance papers) and their expiry dates.
  • Parking: the location and time of each spot you save, and the places you save on the Drive Mode map.
  • Profile: your name, phone, email and photo if you enter them, and your driving licence number and expiry date.
  • Emergency contacts: name, phone number and relationship for each contact you add.
  • AI results: images made with AI Car Modification are saved inside the app.
  • Preferences: settings, map layer, music and radio favourites, links to songs you picked from your phone, and a count of how many AI requests you made today.

Android's automatic backup is turned off for this app, so none of this is copied to your Google account. The flip side is that if you uninstall the app or change phones, your records don't come with you.

2. The AI features

Both AI features use OpenAI's API. Nothing is sent until you tap the button that starts the request.

AI Car Modification

You choose a template and a photo. Before anything is uploaded, the app checks the photo on your phone with Google's ML Kit, for example that it shows a car, or a person for the "Me with a car" templates. That check runs offline. The app then crops the photo, saves it as a new JPEG (which removes location and other EXIF data) and sends it to OpenAI with the template's text instructions. OpenAI returns the edited image.

The result is saved inside the app. It only goes to your gallery (the Pictures/CarPlay AI folder) if you tap Save, and only goes to another app if you tap Share.

Inspection with AI

You pick an area of the car and add one to three photos. The app shrinks them and removes their EXIF data, then sends them to OpenAI twice: first a small copy to confirm the photos show the right part of the car, then a larger copy for the inspection itself. The request asks OpenAI not to store it (store: false).

Each inspection request also includes a random ID created when you installed the app. It is hashed, it isn't linked to your name or Google account, and OpenAI uses it only to detect abuse. Inspection results are kept in memory while the app is open and are never saved.

A few things to know

  • OpenAI handles what it receives under its own privacy policy and API data terms.
  • Don't upload photos with people in them unless they're fine with it.
  • Daily AI limits are counted on your device, not on a server.
  • AI output can be wrong. An inspection result is a first look, not a mechanic's report.
  • The template gallery's preview pictures are downloaded from Unsplash. That download sends no personal information beyond a normal web request.

3. Maps, weather, music and radio

These features ask other services for information. They work like visiting a website: the service sees your IP address plus whatever the feature needs to send.

The public Overpass servers the app may use are run by volunteers and organisations in different countries: overpass-api.de (Germany), overpass.openstreetmap.ru and maps.mail.ru (Russia) and overpass.nchc.org.tw (Taiwan). The app tries them in turn and they receive the same nearby-search request.

From a place's details you can call it, open its website, or share it. These only happen when you tap the button: calling opens your dialer, the website opens in your browser, and sharing uses Android's share sheet with the app you choose.

Songs you add from your phone are played straight from your device. The app only gets access to the files you pick.

4. Google services inside the app

These run in the background as part of the app. We don't add your name, records or photos to anything they send.

  • Google AdMob shows full-screen ads (when you open the app and between some screens). AdMob may use your device's advertising ID, IP address, device model and app usage to show and measure ads. If you are in the EEA, the UK, Switzerland or a US state with a privacy law, the app asks for your choice through Google's consent form before any ad is loaded. If that form was shown to you, you can change your answer at any time in Profile, under Ad privacy choices. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in Android settings.
  • Firebase Analytics collects general usage statistics: when the app is opened, how long sessions last, how often it's used, device model, Android version, app version and approximate country. It uses an app-instance ID, not your name or email.
  • Firebase Crashlytics sends a report when the app crashes: what went wrong in the code, device model, Android version and the app's state at that moment. It helps us fix bugs.
  • Firebase Cloud Messaging gives the app a device token so we can occasionally send a notification, for example about an update. You can turn these off in Android's notification settings.
  • Firebase Remote Config downloads app settings, such as service keys, so we can change them without releasing an update.
  • Google Play Billing: the app includes Google's billing library, but there is nothing to buy in this version. If purchases are added later, Google Play handles payment and we never see your card details.

Google explains how it handles this data in its privacy policy and on how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

5. Permissions

  • Location (precise or approximate, your choice) is asked for the first time you use the speedometer, parking, the Drive Mode map, nearby places or weather. It's used only while the app is open. The app never asks for background location.
  • Notifications (Android 13 and later) is asked when you set up a reminder. Reminders are scheduled on your device and keep working after a restart.
  • Internet is needed for the features in sections 2 to 4.

There are a few permissions the app deliberately doesn't ask for:

  • Camera. Taking a photo opens your phone's camera app, and the app gets only the picture you take.
  • Storage or photos. Android's photo picker and file picker give the app only the items you choose.
  • Contacts. When you add an emergency contact from your phone, Android's contact picker hands over just that contact's name and number.
  • Phone calls. The SOS and Drive Mode call buttons open your dialer with the number filled in. You place the call yourself.

The local emergency numbers (police, rescue, ambulance) come from a list built into the app. The app picks your country from your SIM or mobile network, or your phone's region setting, and none of this leaves the device.

6. Deleting your data

You can edit or delete any record yourself. Delete all data in Profile removes every vehicle, record, document, photo, AI result, contact, saved place, music and radio favourite, SOS number and setting from the app, gives back its access to songs you picked, resets the random install ID and cancels scheduled reminders. It can't be undone. Two things are kept: the built-in AI template gallery, which isn't your data, and today's count of AI requests, so the daily limit can't be reset this way. Uninstalling the app removes everything.

Photos you sent to OpenAI, and data collected by Google's services, sit with those companies. We can't delete it from our side, but we're happy to help you work out who to contact.

7. Children

The app is meant for drivers and vehicle owners. It isn't aimed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them.

8. Security

Data on your device lives in the app's private storage. Every connection the app makes to an online service uses HTTPS. No system is perfectly secure, so keep a screen lock on your phone, especially if you store documents or your licence details in the app.

9. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in Europe or state laws in the US), you may have the right to access, correct or delete personal data, or object to how it's used. Most of your data is under your own control in the app. For anything else, email us and we'll reply within 30 days.
